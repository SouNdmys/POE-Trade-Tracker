## What changed

Three follow-ups decided the morning after 1.1.0, all on the Exchange and Monitor pages.

- **The watch builds its own baseline.** The identity check added in 1.1.0 (a captured book whose
  best rate is ten times off the pair's recent daily median is flagged as a suspect identity)
  compares against daily summaries that, until now, only existed once you had opened the
  Analytics page. Starting a watch now folds the previous days' captures itself, so the check
  works in a session that never visits Analytics. The first start after updating may take a
  moment longer while up to 32 days of captures are folded; later starts skip what is already
  done.
- **The "all kept" chip says how many days it really holds.** The hourly ledger reads at most
  30 days however long you keep hourly rows, and the last range chip used to say "all kept"
  regardless. It now shows the real span — `30 d` when you keep 45 days, `15 d` when you keep
  15 — and falls back to the old wording only in the historical view, where there is no ledger.
  The retention limit and the read cost are unchanged.
- **The hourly columns count back from the sync watermark.** Latest value, volume per hour,
  depth, surge and counterpart used to look at the 48 hours before *now*; the ledger on the
  right always counted back from the last synced hour. When the sync fell behind, the table
  went blank while the ledger still had numbers. Both now count back from the watermark, and
  the header keeps saying how far behind the sync is. The exchange radar is unchanged: it
  judges freshness against the present on purpose, and blanks when the sync is more than a day
  behind — that is noted as an open question rather than papered over.

## If you had this configured

- Nothing you had set behaves differently. The `exchange.hour_retention_days` value keeps its
  meaning; only the chip's label changed.

## Install

Create a folder, extract the zip into it, run `ptt-app.exe`. The zip carries no folder of its
own — twelve files arrive as four loose ones plus `assets\` and `licenses\` — so extracting
straight into Downloads scatters them.

Do not extract into `C:\Program Files`. There is no installer and the program cannot elevate
itself, so the updater will refuse to write there.

Updating from inside the app: press check, then install, then close and reopen the program.
Your settings, calibration and database are untouched.
